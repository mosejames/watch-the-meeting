Meeting Briefing

The September 8th College Park City Council meeting was equal parts theater and financial free-for-all. Council quietly buried a $2.1 million appropriation for the botanical garden's conservatory renovation inside the consent agenda — no discussion, no questions, no debate — pushing Councilman Gay's two-decade pet project ever deeper into public funds. Then came the main event: a 90-minute war over a new destination marketing organization contract that exposed fundamental questions about who controls College Park's tourism dollars, ending in a 3-1 vote with Councilwoman Arnold dissenting over an ordinance she says she was never consulted on. In between, council unanimously approved an elected official security policy with no dollar cap, no budget line, and no accountability mechanism, after Councilwoman McKenzie delivered a 30-minute stream-of-consciousness speech naming nine residents — including a practicing attorney — as security threats for criticizing her leadership. Residents who came to speak about a 261-unit housing development, the comprehensive plan, and the social media civil war tearing the city apart were given three minutes each while council spent unlimited time on its own grievances.
