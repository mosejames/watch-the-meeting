College Park, Georgia

The Narrowing.

There are fewer ways for a resident of College Park to be heard by their city council than there were two years ago. Not one of them closed by accident, and the first closed on this council’s first night in the room.

A new College Park City Council was elected in 2023 and took office in January 2024. At its first meeting it moved fourteen changes to how the city governs itself, in a single vote, on a document that had not been in the packet. One of the fourteen barred Mayor Bianca from commenting on anything the council debates.

Mayor Bianca Motley Broom is the one official every resident of the city votes for, and she is a resident of it herself. What was taken from Mayor Bianca over the following months was taken from the clearest channel residents had — and once that held, the same instruments turned on the room itself: the cameras, the minutes, the clock, the podium, and finally the door.

January – August 2024

The first move

The council elected in 2023 took its seats in January 2024. Its first meeting is where this starts — not a year in, not after some dispute soured, but at the first opportunity. It did not begin with Mayor Bianca's office by accident, and it did not wait.

01
January 2, 2024Limiting the Mayor

On their first night, fourteen changes to how the city governs itself — in one vote.

The council elected the previous year takes its seats this month, and at its first meeting moves fourteen motions as a single item. By Councilman Carn's own account it was not in the packet; it was added on that night. Mayor Bianca says she has not seen it either, and asks for what any ordinary motion gets: a second and a vote on each one. She is told no. She then asks the city attorney whether it is proper to bundle them, and names the motion she says changes the form of government — the council giving itself power over firing city employees. Carn answers in the attorney's place. The attorney never speaks. Eleven seconds into the reading, Mayor Bianca is barred from commenting on anything the council debates.

“I did not have the benefit of receiving these ahead of the meeting. I note there are 14 motions. Normally with a motion there are individual seconds after a motion is made, and then a vote, so I'm hoping that's the intent. The Mayor and Council recently approved as one of our core values transparency. So I hope it's the intent with these, since they didn't get any public notice, that not only will they be read but that they will be acted upon in accordance with the way this document is structured — individually.”Mayor Bianca Motley Broom, asking for fourteen separate votes · January 2, 2024

What it costs youOne vote, so no resident can see which councilmember backed which change. Watch the four clips in order: an ordinary request refused, a legal question left hanging, and the restriction passing anyway.

What happens next, same meeting
02
January 5, 2024Limiting the Mayor

Three days later Mayor Bianca puts the objection on the record.

At a special called meeting Mayor Bianca states her position plainly: the omnibus strips duties the charter gave her office, was never advertised or published the way a charter amendment must be, and reaches her ability to take part at all.

“Motion three, in regard to this omnibus motion that was dealt with at our meeting, among others, infringes my rights under the First Amendment of the US Constitution to participate in these meetings. As a duly elected official, the charter vested me the power to preside over the meetings of this governing body, and the omnibus motion seeks to strip those duties and responsibilities from the office of the mayor.”Mayor Bianca Motley Broom · January 5, 2024

What it costs youMayor Bianca is describing, on day three, the thing the next two years make obvious.

03
January 5, 2024Limiting the Mayor

The staff who helped residents reach the agenda now answer to the council.

Buried in the same omnibus. The legislative assistants had been the way an ordinary resident got something in front of the body — a desk to call, someone whose job was the process rather than any one member's priorities, reporting up through the city manager. The change points them at the council directly. What had been a door into city government becomes staff attached to the people already inside it.

“In terms of legislative assistants reorganization, we're going to have assistants report directly to council and mayor, and administratively to the office of the city manager.”Councilman Joe Carn, reading the amended omnibus · January 5, 2024

What it costs youIf the person who used to help you get on the agenda now works for the members who decide what reaches it, that is not the same job.

04
January 16, 2024Limiting themselves

Two weeks later the rule catches their own Mayor Pro Tem.

The Mayor Pro Tem, presiding in her place, starts to make a comment and is stopped by a point of order citing the rule they had just written. They had aimed it at one person. It attaches to the chair, so it silences whoever is holding the gavel. Read what they tell him to do about it, because it matters six days later: hand the gavel back to Mayor Bianca, and then you may speak.

I would like to make a comment and then entertain—Point of order. While you're presiding you may not make a comment. The chair cannot make a comment. So when it's time to vote, you can hand the gavel back to the mayor and then she'll call for a vote, or you make comment at that time once she's presiding once more.Mayor Pro Tem, then a council member raising the point of order · January 16, 2024

What it costs youA council that reaches for a rule to quiet one officeholder, without noticing it will land on the next person in that seat, is not being careful with your government.

05
January 22, 2024Limiting the Mayor

So Mayor Bianca does exactly what they told him to do.

A special meeting to consider undoing the council's firing of the city manager. Mayor Bianca wants to speak to it, so she uses the way out they described and hands off the chair. The Mayor Pro Tem declines. A voice reminds him he may decline. He declines again. She asks the city attorney, on the record, whether she is permitted to speak at all. His answer closes it: she may pass the gavel only if a colleague is inclined to accept, and that is their decision, not hers.

Has the Mayor Pro Tem accepted the gavel?I decline.Mayor Bianca, then Mayor Pro Tem Roderick Gay · January 22, 2024

What it costs youA way out that only your opponents can open is not a way out.

What happens next, same meeting
06
January 22, 2024Limiting the Mayor

All four refuse to take it.

Mayor Bianca goes down the line. Gay declines. Carn does not wait to be asked — “No, you will not.” Arnold declines. McKenzie declines and gives a reason: she made the motion, so she cannot preside. Four seats, four refusals, inside a minute. Playing by their rules requires one of them to be willing, and not one of them is.

I would turn those over to Council member Carn.No, you will not.Mayor Bianca, then Councilman Joe Carn · January 22, 2024

What it costs youThis is the whole machine, and it runs on four words.

What happens next, same meeting
07
February 5, 2024Limiting the public

Special meetings stop being Mayor Bianca's to call — and she says who else that costs.

An ordinance takes Mayor Bianca's authority to call a special meeting and drops the number of members needed from four to three. Arguing against it, she makes the connection this page is about, out loud, in February 2024.

“Changing the requirement for special meetings removes authority from the mayor to call special meetings, and then decreases the number required from four to three. I don't think this is a good idea. Special meetings infer that there is urgency. It also removes a critical component of feedback from the public — council is taking actions and the public doesn't have a voice in that regard.”Mayor Bianca Motley Broom · February 5, 2024

What it costs youThree members can now call a meeting without her, and special meetings carry no public comment. The business gets done in the room you were not invited to.

08
February 19, 2024Limiting the Mayor

Asking the city manager a question is ruled out of order.

During the city manager's report Mayor Bianca asks him about the council retreat, and a point of order is raised that the question itself is improper. Not the answer, not the topic — the asking.

Point of order — we're out of order here, this is the city manager's report.I had a question for him during his report. We haven't moved on to the next item yet. So, sir, I do have questions.A council member, then Mayor Bianca Motley Broom · February 19, 2024

What it costs youEvery question Mayor Bianca puts to staff is a question a resident does not have to file a records request to get answered. This is the first test of whether that can be stopped.

Eighteen months on, they use it again — and this time it sticks.

February was the test. This is the refinement. Mayor Bianca raises a utility item and is told, on the record, that discussing items is out of order of how we do things. The ruling has stopped being about any particular question.

August 2024

The censure, and the cleared room

A Friday meeting called to censure Mayor Bianca ended with police clearing the residents who came to watch it, and two of them in handcuffs. Whatever the first eight months were about, after this night they were about everyone.

12
August 9, 2024Limiting the public

They censure Mayor Bianca. Then police clear the people who came to watch.

A specially called Friday meeting to censure her. When the gallery protested, the interim city manager directed police to eject them and two people were led away in handcuffs. The Georgia Attorney General's office asked the city for answers. The council finished the meeting without an audience, then let the public back in.

“The Georgia attorney general's office is demanding answers from College Park officials after the gallery was cleared by police during a specially called meeting last Friday. Two people were led away in handcuffs as the interim city manager told police to eject the residents from the room. The meeting was held to censure Mayor Bianca Motley Broom for what the city council says was ongoing disruptive behavior.”A television news report, played into the record · August 9, 2024

What it costs youUp to this night the restrictions were procedural and aimed at one office. This is the night they were enforced on a room full of residents.

April 2024 – September 2026

Turning on the room

The same instruments, pointed outward. Cameras off. The record thinned. A clock on the room, then a shorter clock, then no way to lend your minutes to a neighbor, then no way to write in at all.

14
April 15, 2024Limiting the public

The budget hearings go off the air.

A motion moves the budget hearings into the executive conference room and takes them off the broadcast. The room stays open and the clerk still takes minutes. The cameras and the stream go off. The reason given is the cost of streaming.

That's probably where the best — would be the better place to do it.Are you making a motion to not televise the budget hearings?I am.The chair, confirming the motion with its maker · April 15, 2024

What it costs youIf you work nights, have small children, or have no ride to City Hall, the budget stops being something you can watch.

A unanimous vote holds closing remarks to five minutes. That vote is law, it has never been repealed, and it is not followed. Meetings still run past it. The captioner drops the late procedural stretch where the vote happened, so the ordinance text carries this one.

What happened

With the cameras off, a resident sat in the room and broadcast the budget hearing from her own phone. Others complained. The council gave ground and ran the feed back into the main chamber. It is the only reversal on this page, and no rule produced it.

What happened

After residents turned out to speak against an item, the order of business was rearranged so comment followed the meeting rather than preceding it. It held for one night, and comment sits near the top of the agenda today. It belongs here as an attempt, and as the first time the agenda itself was used on the public.

18
October 10, 2024Limiting the public

Thirty minutes for the room. Six for everyone who could not be in it.

A McKenzie motion sets the public comment period at 30 minutes in person and 6 minutes for emailed comment — and the six are inside a 36-minute total, not added to it. Carn votes in favor, Gay abstains. The reason given is a fair one: emailed comments had not been read at all, and this guaranteed them a slot.

Council member McKenzie, what was your motion?The motion was that we would adhere to 30 minutes here for in-person comments and 6 minutes for email comments.What was happening is email comments weren't getting read at all, so we said we would make sure that we would give 6 minutes for email comments.Mayor Bianca, then Councilwoman Jamelle McKenzie · October 10, 2024

What it costs youSix minutes for every emailed comment from every resident, combined. Sold as a floor. It works as a ceiling, and thirteen months later the ceiling goes to zero.

The minutes stop recording what anyone said.

From this meeting on, the minutes posted to the city website carry motions and votes and nothing else. Nothing is announced. You find it by comparing documents: the earlier sets record the discussion speaker by speaker, the later ones do not. The verbatim record still exists — inside the *next* meeting's agenda packet.

What it costs youIt was not deleted, it was moved somewhere no ordinary resident would think to look. To read what your councilmember said in September you must know to open the October packet. Nobody tells you that, and “it's still available” is exactly what the move buys them.

About this record

Everything above comes from the City of College Park’s own meeting video, posted by the city to its own channel. Where a moment can be located, there is a still from the meeting and a player that opens at the second it was said. Nothing loads from YouTube until you press play.

The entries set in dark are the ones the city’s video cannot show you. Several of those meetings were posted without captions of any kind, so the moment cannot be found by searching a transcript — among them the censure and the cleared room, the motion to override the chair, and the thirty-minute hearing cap. Two dates are partial uploads: five minutes posted for a full regular session on May 5, 2025, and an hour and nineteen minutes across two files for June 17, 2024. One meeting, the budget hearing of May 9, 2024, has no city video at all, because the council had voted not to stream it.

The meetings that are hardest to follow are not a random sample of the calendar. What the city captions, keeps and posts is a fair question to put to it, and the May 9 recording is worth an open records request.

Quotes are taken from the meetings’ automatic captions, cleaned up for punctuation and checked against the audio. Where an upload carries two English caption tracks, only the original is used: the machine-retranslated track on these meetings invents words, and on September 21, 2026 it rendered “Motion dies for lack of a second” as “The offer was rejected due to lack of offers.”

Every limit here that passed lands on the public or on Mayor Bianca. The one that would have landed on the council could not find a second.
