Between January 2024 and September 2026 the College Park City Council changed the rules of its own meetings more than two dozen times. Taken one at a time, each change reads as housekeeping. Taken in order, they run in one direction.

This page puts them in order. Where the city posted video and the moment could be located in the transcript, there is a receipt: a still from the meeting and a player that starts at the second it was said. Where there is no video, or no captions to search, the entry says so rather than leaving a gap you cannot see.

Act One · January 2024 – April 2025

What they did to the Mayor

The Mayor of College Park is elected citywide. Every resident votes for her. Over sixteen months the Council removed, one at a time, the ways that office could act on their behalf: her voice in debate, her staff, her power to call a meeting, her right to ask a question of her own city manager.

01
January 2, 2024

Fourteen separate actions, taken in a single vote.

At the first meeting of 2024, the Council moved fourteen changes to how the city governs itself as one block. There was no item-by-item debate and no separate vote on any one of them. Several of the fourteen reduced the Mayor's authority.

“I'm reading them all sir, we're doing them collectively, we're voting collectively.”Councilman Joe Carn · January 2, 2024

What it cost youNobody could see which changes their councilmember supported, because there was only one vote to see.

02
January 2, 2024

The Mayor may no longer comment on what the Council is debating.

Motion three of the omnibus. While presiding, the Mayor is barred from commenting or expressing an opinion on the matter in front of the body. She still runs the meeting. She may not speak to what is in it.

“While presiding over the deliberations of the city council in accordance with the charter, the mayor shall be prohibited from commenting or expressing opinions on the matters being discussed.”Read into the record as motion three · January 2, 2024

What it cost youThe one official every resident in the city voted for can no longer argue for you in the room.

03
January 5, 2024

Three days later, she reads her objection into the record.

At a special called meeting, the Mayor puts her legal position on the record: the omnibus strips duties the charter gave her office, was never advertised or published as a charter amendment requires, and infringes her ability to participate at all.

“Motion three, in regard to this omnibus motion that was dealt with at our meeting, among others, infringes my rights under the First Amendment of the US Constitution to participate in these meetings. As a duly elected official, the charter vested me the power to preside over the meetings of this governing body, and the omnibus motion seeks to strip those duties and responsibilities from the office of the mayor.”Mayor Bianca Motley Broom · January 5, 2024

What it cost youThis is the clearest statement on the record of what was taken, said by the person it was taken from.

04
January 5, 2024

Legislative staff now report to the Council.

An amendment read into the same omnibus reorganizes the legislative assistants so they report to council and mayor, and administratively to the city manager.

“In terms of legislative assistants reorganization, we're going to have assistants report directly to council and mayor, and administratively to the office of the city manager.”Councilman Joe Carn, reading the amended omnibus · January 5, 2024

What it cost youThe staff who help a resident get an item in front of the body now answer to the body.

05
January 16, 2024

The rule immediately binds whoever holds the gavel.

Two weeks later the Mayor Pro Tem, presiding, tries to make a comment and is stopped by a point of order using the new rule. The restriction was written to sideline the Mayor. It attached to the chair itself.

I would like to make a comment and then entertain—Point of order. While you're presiding you may not make a comment. The chair cannot make a comment. So when it's time to vote, you can hand the gavel back to the mayor and then she'll call for a vote, or you make comment at that time once she's presiding once more.Mayor Pro Tem, then a council member raising the point of order · January 16, 2024

What it cost youDemonstrates the rule is structural, not personal. Whoever runs the meeting is mute on its contents.

06
January 22, 2024

So she tries to hand off the gavel in order to speak.

If the presiding officer cannot speak, the obvious workaround is to stop presiding. The Mayor relinquishes the gavel to the Mayor Pro Tem so that she can join the discussion as a member.

“I've relinquished presiding duties to the Mayor Pro Tem as I wish to speak to this conversation.”Mayor Bianca Motley Broom · January 22, 2024
07
January 22, 2024

All four decline to take it.

Each of the four councilmembers in turn refuses the gavel. With no one willing to preside, the Mayor must keep the gavel, and keeping the gavel means she cannot speak. The workaround is closed the same night it is tried.

“I decline.”Mayor Pro Tem Gay, then Carn, Arnold and McKenzie in turn · January 22, 2024

What it cost youTwo words, four times: I decline. That is the whole mechanism.

08
February 5, 2024

Calling a special meeting is no longer the Mayor's to do.

An ordinance removes the Mayor's authority to call a special meeting and lowers the number of members needed from four to three. Arguing against it, the Mayor makes the connection this entire page is about, out loud, in February 2024.

“Changing the requirement for special meetings removes authority from the mayor to call special meetings, and then decreases the number required from four to three. I don't think this is a good idea. Special meetings infer that there is urgency. It also removes a critical component of feedback from the public — council is taking actions and the public doesn't have a voice in that regard.”Mayor Bianca Motley Broom · February 5, 2024

What it cost youA meeting can now be called by three members without the Mayor, and special meetings carry no public comment.

09
February 19, 2024

A question to the city manager is ruled out of order.

During the city manager's report the Mayor asks him a question about the council retreat. A point of order is raised that the question itself is out of order.

Point of order — we're out of order here, this is the city manager's report.I had a question for him during his report. We haven't moved on to the next item yet. So, sir, I do have questions.A council member, then Mayor Bianca Motley Broom · February 19, 2024

What it cost youIf the presiding officer cannot question staff during the staff report, no one is questioning staff on your behalf.

The hinge · August 2024

The censure, and the cleared room

This is where the two halves meet. A meeting called to censure the Mayor ended with police clearing the residents who came to watch it. From here the restrictions stop being about one officeholder.

13
August 9, 2024

The Council censures the Mayor. Police clear the residents who came to watch.

A specially called Friday meeting was held to censure the Mayor. When the public in the gallery protested, the interim city manager directed police to eject them. Two people were led away in handcuffs. The Georgia Attorney General's office asked the city for answers. The Council then continued the meeting without an audience.

“The Georgia attorney general's office is demanding answers from College Park officials after the gallery was cleared by police during a specially called meeting last Friday. Two people were led away in handcuffs as the interim city manager told police to eject the residents from the room. The meeting was held to censure Mayor Bianca Motley Broom for what the city council says was ongoing disruptive behavior.”A television news report, played into the record · August 9, 2024

What it cost youThis is the night the two halves become one. The censure is aimed at the Mayor. The handcuffs are for the public.

14
August 19, 2024

Ten days later, the Mayor speaks from the public podium.

Unable to speak from the dais, the Mayor leaves it, walks to the podium the public uses, and gives her name and address like any other resident signing up to speak. The captions for this meeting never render that line, so the receipt is not pinned.

What it cost youThe chief elected officer of the city had to queue up as a member of the public to be heard in her own chamber.

Act Two · April 2024 – September 2026

What they did to everyone else

The same tools, pointed outward. Cameras off. Minutes thinned. A clock on the room, then a shorter clock, then no way to pool your minutes with a neighbor, then no email at all.

15
April 15, 2024

The budget hearings are taken off the air.

A motion is made to move the budget hearings into the executive conference room and not televise them. The room stays open and minutes are still taken, but the cameras and the stream are switched off. The stated reason is the cost of streaming.

That's probably where the best — would be the better place to do it.Are you making a motion to not televise the budget hearings?I am.The chair, confirming the motion with its maker · April 15, 2024

What it cost youIf you work nights, have kids, or have no ride to City Hall, the budget stops being something you can watch.

16
May 6, 2024

Closing remarks are capped at five minutes.

The Council votes unanimously to limit closing remarks to five minutes. That vote is law and has never been repealed. It is also, by the Council's own practice, not followed. The four-and-a-half hour meeting is captioned, but the captioner drops the late procedural exchange, so the ordinance text is the better citation here.

May 9, 2024

A resident streams the hearing on her own phone.

With the cameras off, a resident sat in the room and livestreamed the budget hearing herself. Residents complained. The Council conceded and piped the feed back into the main chamber. It is the only rollback on this page, and a resident with a phone forced it.

Comment was reordered to follow the business of the meeting. It was a single night, not a permanent change, and comment sits near the top of the agenda today. It belongs here as an attempt, not as a standing rule.

19
October 10, 2024

Emailed comment is ranked last.

The chair sets out the order: people who signed in, then people who arrive after the meeting starts, then emailed comments. The stated reason is to put those who showed up in person first, which is a fair goal. The effect is that email is read only if time remains.

“So the priority, as we had determined, was the people that came for public comment that signed in; the people that have come after the sign-in, as the meeting has commenced, second priority; then the email comments.”The chair, explaining the tiers · October 10, 2024

What it cost youOnce a clock is put on the whole period, last in line means never reached.

The posted minutes stop recording what was said.

From this meeting forward, the minutes posted to the city website record actions only. Nothing is said aloud about the change. You can see it by comparing the documents: earlier sets carry speaker-by-speaker discussion, later ones carry motions and votes. Kept here deliberately as the before picture. In November 2025 residents yielded time to each other and the chair simply counted it. Six additional minutes, no objection, no procedure.

23
November 3, 2025

The thirty-minute cap, enforced.

The in-person comment period is capped at thirty minutes. Here it runs out on the record, with people still waiting, and the balance of the period is given to emailed comments instead.

“I would like to address the public. The in-person public comment is allowed for 30 minutes and we have exhausted the 30 minute time. The remainder of the six minutes is for emailed public comments.”Mayor Bianca Motley Broom, announcing the cutoff · November 3, 2025

What it cost youEveryone still standing in line that night went home without speaking.

The channel that was ranked last in October 2024 is removed entirely thirteen months later. The resolution is sponsored by the city attorney.

“Approve a resolution regarding City of College Park public comment policy. This item is sponsored by city attorney.”Clerk, reading Resolution 2025-44 · November 17, 2025

What it cost youIf you cannot get to City Hall on a Monday night, you no longer have a way in.

What has no video, and why that matters

20moments pinned to an exact second, with a still from the meeting
11posted, but with no captions to search or no partial upload to search in
1meeting for which the city made no recording at all

The city posts its meetings to its own YouTube channel, and most of this page rests on that record. But the record is not evenly available:

  • May 9, 2024 has no video because the Council had voted not to stream the budget hearings. The only recording known to exist is a resident’s phone.
  • Six meetings are posted with no captions on any upload, so the moment cannot be found by searching a transcript. They include the censure and the cleared room, the motion to override the chair, the night a resident was escorted out, the yielding ban, and the thirty-minute hearing cap.
  • Three meetings are captioned, but the moment is not in the transcript at all.
  • Two dates are partial uploads: five minutes posted for a full regular session on May 5, 2025, and an hour and nineteen minutes across two files for June 17, 2024.

The meetings that are hardest to search are not a random sample of the calendar. That is worth an answer from the city about how it captions, retains and posts its meeting video, and it is worth an open records request for any recording of May 9, 2024.

One more caution for anyone checking this work. Several of these uploads carry two English caption tracks, and where both exist the plain en track is a machine retranslation that invents words: on September 21, 2026 it rendered “Motion dies for lack of a second” as “The offer was rejected due to lack of offers.” Quotes here are taken from the original track and checked against the audio.

Every restriction on this page that passed applies to the public or to the Mayor. The one that would have applied to the Council could not find a second.
